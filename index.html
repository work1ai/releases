Pain 01

You don't know where it's safe to let the machine decide

The question isn't "can we automate this?" It's "what happens when it goes wrong?" Without documented risk thresholds and human supervision checkpoints, every automation is a liability. Most AI agencies don't have a framework for this. We built one.

Pain 02

Your decisions live in email threads and tribal knowledge

Five systems give five different answers. Approvals go to whoever picked up the phone. Nobody can articulate the actual rule — they just know it when they see it. AI can't automate what nobody has documented. We document it first.

Pain 03

The AI industry is selling growth tools when you need operational ones

Chatbots. Social automation. AI-generated content. These are fine — but they're not where SMEs lose time and money. The real leverage is in your operations: approvals, routing, intake, coordination, quality control. That's where we work.

Pain 04

You've been burned by implementations that promised more than they delivered

Big proposals, impressive demos, then months of rework and scope creep. By the time it launched it didn't match what was sold — and the vendor had already moved on. Now you're cautious, and rightly so. The problem isn't that you're hesitant. It's that the industry made hesitation the rational choice.

Common questions

The questions we hear on almost every first call.

We've tried something like this before and it never made it past the pilot. How is this different?+
That's the norm, not the exception — 67% of organizations cite data quality issues as their top AI readiness challenge. Usually it's not the technology that stalls, it's that nobody documented the decision logic or risk thresholds before building, so nobody trusts the thing enough to expand it. That's the entire reason the Roadmap phase exists before any build starts: you get the decision logic, risk maps, and escalation rules on paper first. If a Live Pilot makes sense afterward, it's a separately scoped step you can say yes or no to — not a sunk cost you're already inside of. Source: LinkedIn, 2026
What happens to our data — who can see it, and where does it live?+
Data security is consistently the single most-cited concern small businesses raise about AI adoption, ahead of cost or job impact — so we don't leave it implicit. Every workflow we map gets a documented data-risk assessment as part of your Risk Maps deliverable: what data the automation touches, what's sensitive, and where a human needs to see something before it moves. Nothing is assumed safe by default. If a step involves data you'd want a person to review first, that's built in as a circuit breaker, not an afterthought. Source: Constant Contact / Statista, SMB AI Adoption Survey
Will this replace our team, or put jobs at risk?+
Most small business owners don't think it will, and we're built around that view: 64% of small businesses don't believe AI will replace jobs in their industry within the next five years. We build toward the same outcome deliberately — we automate coordination and busywork (routing, approvals, status-chasing), not judgment calls, and every automation has a documented point where a named person reviews it. Our own deliverables ship as documentation your team can audit, and we measure our own success by whether your people actually adopt what we build, not just whether it got deployed. Source: Comerica Small Business Pulse Index, 2025
Will this actually work with the systems we already use?+
This is the single most common technical objection we hear, and it's a fair one — 47% of small businesses run into integration headaches trying to bolt AI onto legacy systems. It's also exactly why the Roadmap starts with mapping your actual systems of record before we recommend anything. We're not selling a platform you have to migrate to; we document what you already run, where the data actually lives, and design around it. If something genuinely won't integrate cleanly, that comes out in the Roadmap — not six weeks into a build. Source: First Movers, AI Consulting for Small Business, 2026
How much does this cost, and will the price change once we're in the door?+
Every Roadmap is scoped to your company size and the number of systems of record we need to map, so we quote your exact number on the discovery call — before you commit to anything. The Roadmap itself is fixed-scope and fixed-price: whatever we quote is what you pay, with no scope creep added mid-engagement. If we later scope a Live Pilot or Full Implementation, that's a separate, clearly priced step you decide on with the Roadmap findings already in hand — never a number that moves after you've already said yes. Book a 30-minute call and we'll walk you through the number for your business.
